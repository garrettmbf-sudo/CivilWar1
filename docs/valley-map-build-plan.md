# Valley campaign map: build plan (scale, shape, rendering)

**Status:** PLAN + PROTOTYPE. No game tile or .sav has been made and nothing has been tested in Hex of Steel. The grid is flat-top odd-q from `src/hex_utils.py` (in-game corner test still pending).

**Kernstown stays as it is:** the 250-yd standalone test scenario on `kernstown-map`. It is not re-rendered, and its files are only read.

## 1. How the scale is chosen

File size does **not** decide the scale. The scale comes from Hex of Steel's rules and from 1862 tactics. Packaging is solved afterwards (§4).

### 1.1 Hex of Steel rules that depend on hex size
All from the [official manual, Oct 2025](https://shared.fastly.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/1240630/manuals/1e929c3c9d09670cb450bdf6272b9522dfb4bc5b/Manual.pdf) unless marked:

| Mechanic | What the manual says | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attack range | Whole hexes, with no line-of-sight or terrain blocking. Base-game artillery ranges are **1 to 4**. | Muskets are always range 1 (adjacent). Artillery has to fit 1–4 for stock behaviour; ranges above 4 aren't documented. |
| Visibility (fog of war) | **1 hex** by default, **2 for recon** units. | Infantry sees 1 hex and cavalry set as recon sees 2. Per-unit editing is plausible from the unit editor but not verified. |
| Action points | 2 per turn; a unit can move once and attack once. Recon can move twice. | Each unit gets one combat exchange per turn, so a battle needs enough turns. Cavalry fits recon (move twice, ZoC-exempt, +15% to friendly attacks next to it). |
| Movement points | MP per turn. Road costs 1 per hex, plain 2, forest 3. Example: "on road only … up to 9 tiles per turn". Horse-drawn sets max MP 3. | Road march per turn = MP. Stock values are about 3–9. |
| Zone of control | Optional (v7.0.0+). Applies to adjacent hexes; not applied by recon, commanders, militia, units below 30% HP or with no ammo. | ZoC reaches one hex. |
| Commanders | Bonus radius in tiles (manual example: 7). | Jackson's command reach is set in hexes. |
| Entrenchment, supportive fire, retreat, trains, bridge demolition | All per turn and per hex. | Trains extend MP, which fits the Manassas Gap RR and Virginia Central RR. |
| Turn length | Scenario **days per turn** (0 = time frozen; 1 day and 7 days appear in sources). No hour setting found (see `docs/hos-modding-notes.md`). | Hourly turns are **not native**. Options: days-per-turn = 0 with turn-numbered events and an abstract clock, or a Harmony clock. See §6. |
| Stacking | Not described; swapping units in hexes suggests one land unit per hex. | Needs verifying in-game. |

I found no assembly with game logic in the repo: `docs/3574617535` holds only Harmony DLLs and empty BinaryFormatter lists. No mechanics were taken from code.

### 1.2 1862 facts used
Standard ranges from common references; listed for review:
- smoothbore musket ~100 yd effective;
- rifle-musket ~200–300 yd effective;
- canister ~400 yd;
- 12-pdr Napoleon ~1,600 yd max;
- 3-inch rifle / 10-pdr Parrott ~1,800–2,000 yd max;
- brigade in line ~400–800 yd front;
- march 15–20 mi/day, Jackson up to 25–30;
- column ~2.5 mph including halts, cavalry ~4 mph.

### 1.4 Recommendation: 500 yd per hex; 1-hour turns in contact (provisional)

**Correction:** HoS turns count whole days, so 1-hour turns need either the frozen-time setting (days per turn = 0) plus a turn-numbered clock, or a Harmony mod. The campaign-length options in §6 avoid about 1,100 hourly turns.
- **Artillery.** 500 yd is the finest scale where artillery fits the documented 1–4 range: canister and short range at 1, Napoleon 3, rifled guns 4. That keeps smoothbore and rifled batteries distinct, which they aren't at 1000 yd (both 2). At 400 yd rifled guns need range 5, which isn't documented.
- **Infantry fire.** Adjacent hexes are 500 yd centre to centre, so musket fights resolve between brigades about 250–500 yd apart. That is close to real engagement distances. At 750–1000 yd, "adjacent" would be well beyond musket range.
- **Brigades and ZoC.** One brigade in line fills about one hex (0.8–1.6). With one unit per hex, a brigade is a counter and a division front is 3–4 hexes. ZoC of one 500-yd hex is a believable skirmish screen.
- **Spotting.** Infantry sees 500 yd, which suits a wooded, rolling valley under the fog of war. Cavalry as recon sees 1,000 yd and moves twice, the closest stock fit for vedette screens. Longer vedette sight needs per-unit visibility, if the editor allows it, or a later Harmony patch. That is open question 3.
- **Turn length.** 1-hour turns give infantry about **9 road MP**, which matches the manual's stock "9 tiles per turn" road example. Off-road works out to about 4–5 hexes (1.25 mi/h). Cavalry gets about 14 MP and two moves as recon.
  - A 20-mile day is about 8 marching turns.
  - A Kernstown-length fight (about 3–5 h) lasts 3–5 turns, enough for attack, support, retreat and pursuit with one attack per unit per turn.
  - With 2-hour turns, battles would last only 2–3 turns and infantry would need 18 MP.
- **Cost.** About 14 daylight turns a day, roughly **1,100 turns** for 23 Mar–9 Jun.
  - Night: no night turns, or one "night" turn per day. This depends on whether the editor can advance the date per turn (unverified).
  - Battle scenarios (McDowell, Front Royal and so on) start on their own dates, so they are short.
  - If the full campaign is too long, 750 yd with 2-hour turns is the fallback. Its MP (12 inf / 19 cav) and artillery (2/3) are workable, but musket adjacency is coarser.

## 2. Map shape: Piedmont removed
Data lives in `scenarios/valley_campaign_1862/data/`. Everything is PROVISIONAL with citations to verify.
- `map_shape.json`: a 22-vertex polygon (mask).
  - **East edge:** about 5–8 km east of the Blue Ridge crest, so each gap road runs down the east slope to an exit hex. Culpeper, Warrenton, Gordonsville and Charlottesville are tested to be outside.
  - **West edge:** keeps Romney, Moorefield, Petersburg, Franklin, McDowell and Monterey.
  - **South edge:** just below Lexington.
- `blue_ridge_passes.csv`: 12 gaps, with 1862 road, exit destination, role, confidence, uncertainty and sources.
  - Harpers Ferry (water gap)
  - Keys'
  - Snicker's
  - Ashby's
  - Manassas
  - Chester
  - Thornton's
  - Fisher's
  - Swift Run
  - Brown's
  - Jarman's
  - Rockfish
- `east_exit_edges.csv`: one east-edge exit per pass. Each exit is planned as a controlled entry/exit point for HoS reinforcement tiles and a later "Jackson leaves to defend Richmond" mechanic:
  - via Brown's Gap → Mechum's River Station (Virginia Central RR) for the June 1862 historical route;
  - via Rockfish Gap → Virginia Central RR;
  - via Swift Run Gap → Gordonsville.
- `western_roads.csv`:
  - Northwestern Turnpike (Winchester–Romney)
  - Moorefield–Wardensville–Strasburg (Frémont's late-May route)
  - South Branch road (Moorefield–Petersburg–Franklin)
  - Franklin–Harrisonburg over Shenandoah Mountain
  - **Staunton–Parkersburg Turnpike** (Staunton–Buffalo Gap–Shenandoah Mountain–Bull Pasture Mountain–McDowell–Monterey)
  - Valley Turnpike south to Lexington

Coordinates are approximate modern values and need checking against USGS GNIS and Hotchkiss. The history columns cite only works known to exist (OR Ser. ## 3. Rendering: primary plan (revised with the Kernstown bot, 17 Oct)
Unique slices for all 123k hexes don't scale: about 100 GB even after optimisation. Primary plan:
1. **Edge-matched library as the base.** Corner-matched area tiles, 8–16 variants per class (open/fields, woods, hill, mountain slope, ridge, wet meadow, orchard). Field boundaries are drawn off-grid. Each hex picks its variant deterministically (`scripts/valley_tile_plan.py`) and identical neighbours are forbidden.
2. **Modulation from a low-res painted basemap.** A continuous Kernstown-style basemap is painted at low resolution: DEM hillshade, 1862 land-cover masks, March palette. Each hex samples its tint and brightness, snapped to a few levels (e.g. 4 brightness × 2 tint). Pre-generated library variants exist for each level. This gives large-scale continuity (slopes, light, field patterns) without unique tiles. No runtime tinting is needed, because HoS only draws the named PNG.
3. **Unique painted tiles only where the art carries information:**
   - towns and villages, gaps and crossings;
   - the six battlefields plus Lexington/VMI;
   - the **Valley Pike and river corridors** (North/South Fork, North River, Cedar Creek…).

   They are painted continuously and sliced, Kernstown v0.4.0 style.
4. **Dedupe** tiles that come out byte-identical after quantisation (hash). Mountain forest interiors collapse to the library.
5. **Last resort:** sector maps (split the campaign into linked scenarios, §6 option A).

**Estimates at 2000² PNG, 0.82 MB/tile** (Kernstown bot's v0.4.1 encoding):

| Item | 500 yd | 750 yd |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Battlefield cores (6 × ~30 km²) | ~1,000 | ~440 |
| Towns, villages, gaps, crossings | ~250 | ~120 |
| Pike and river corridors (~400 corridor-miles, 1 hex wide) | ~1,400 | ~940 |
| Lexington/VMI | ~10 | ~5 |
| **Unique painted** | **~2,650** | **~1,500** |
| Library (10 classes × 12 variants + linear/transition ~300) | ~420 | ~420 |
| Modulation variants (area classes × ~8 tint/brightness levels × 3 kept variants) | ~240 | ~240 |
| **Total unique tiles** | **~3,300** | **~2,150** |
| **Disk** | **~2.7 GB** | **~1.8 GB** |

These are planning estimates. Corridor and battlefield footprints get measured once the GIS layers exist.

**Style guards** (binding, from the Kernstown bot):
- **Grid:** no visible hex grid in field or wood edges.
- **Palette:** March 1862. Tan and brown fields, bare grey-brown woods. Faint green only for wet meadows and wheat. The Valley Pike is pale limestone.
- **Content:** no anachronisms. Buildings sit square to streets.
- **Relief:** the valley floor stays flat except Massanutten, the Blue Ridge and the western ridges.
- **Camera:** a consistent ~75–80° north-facing view.
- **Look:** an understated classic wargame style (*Stonewall Jackson's Way*, *Terrible Swift Sword*), with sparse labels.

**Earlier prototype result** (PR #10): reusing Kernstown slices outside their original position doubles the edge mismatch (8.3 → 16.7). Fading rims to a class colour fixes the number but shows a hex ring. So library tiles are painted with shared corner textures, never cut from Kernstown.

Kernstown itself stays as-is: the 250-yd standalone test.

## 4. File-size options, ranked (look-preserving first)
The tile format must be 2000×2000 PNG (official tutorial).

| Rank | Option | Saving | Visual cost | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Library + modulation + unique only at key sites** (§3) | ~100 GB → ~2–3 GB | Low, if the library is painted well. Interior variety comes from modulation. | Estimate |
| 2 | **Polygon trimming** (Piedmont removed) | ~45% fewer hexes than the bounding grid | None | `scripts/valley_map_shape.py` |
| 3 | **7-bit RGB + oxipng** (`-o 3 --strip safe --nc`) | Kernstown bot (v0.4.1): 1.15 → 0.82 MB/tile (**-29%**), worst ΔE 1.2, SSIM 0.9965. **The user picked this.** | Invisible | Kernstown bot; my check below |
| 4 | **Dedupe identical slices** after quantisation | Large in uniform forest/mountain, ~0 on the valley floor | None | Not yet measured on Valley data |
| 5 | **Stock or library tiles for featureless interior hexes** (forested mountainsides) | Up to the share of mountain hexes (≈30–40% of the polygon; to be measured) | Medium if stock art clashes with the style. Prefer our library. | Plan |
| 6 | 6-bit RGB | -44% | Slight banding | Kernstown bot |
| 7 | pngquant 256 colours | -36% (Kernstown bot); -44% on my 11 tiles | **Colour spikes (ΔE ~15; max channel error 133 here) and the alpha edge changes** (84k alpha pixels changed in 11 tiles). Not acceptable. | Kernstown bot + my check |
| 8 | 5-bit RGB | -63% | Visible banding | Kernstown bot |
| — | 1024² / 512² tiles | ~4–10× | Unknown | **Unverified; needs an in-game test.** Not assumed. |

**My check** on 11 real v0.4.0 release tiles (`SV_K_20_2*`, part2 zip, 17.7 MB total, 1.61 MB/tile, not optimised):
- oxipng alone: **-29%**, lossless;
- 7-bit RGB + oxipng `--nc`: **-46%**, max error 1/255, mean 0.5, alpha unchanged;
- pngquant 256: -44%, with the colour and alpha problems above.

The bot's smaller oxipng-only gain (-1.5%) applies to its v0.4.1 base, which was already optimised.

## 6. Campaign length: options instead of ~1,100 hourly turns
Window: 23 Mar–9 Jun 1862 = **79 days**.

**Option A: linked scenarios (mostly native).**
- An operational map at 1 day per turn, at a coarser companion scale (1–2 mi/hex), plus separate battle maps at 500 yd (Kernstown is already one).
- Native custom campaigns carry core units, with stats and history, from scenario to scenario. Limits: the core-unit count and the fixed scenario tree; branching on an outcome is UNKNOWN.
- **About 79 operational turns + 6 battles × ~8 = ~130 turns.**
- Cost: a second, coarser map, and battles are pre-placed rather than emerging anywhere.

**Option B: one 500-yd map with variable turn length (needs Harmony).**
- Native setting days per turn = 0 (frozen). A Harmony patch on `TurnManager.NextTurn` keeps the real clock:
  - **operational turns of 4–6 h** when no enemy is within N hexes, with MP scaled up for that turn;
  - **1-hour contact turns** once forces are within N hexes;
  - **one night encampment turn** per day: pickets and units recover. Unit quality decides whether a night move is noticed. Night combat carries a heavy penalty (later feature).
- **About 79 × (2–3 day turns + 1 night) ≈ 240–320, plus ~15 contact days × ~8 extra hourly turns ≈ 120 → ~360–440 turns.**
- Cost: Harmony work and testing; the AI must cope with MP changing per turn.

**Option C: one 500-yd map, native only.**
- Days per turn = 0, with an abstract clock driven by turn-numbered events: 1 turn = 3 h, 4 daylight turns + 1 night turn per day.
- Infantry road MP of about 26 (editable unit stat) and cavalry about 42.
- **About 79 × 5 ≈ 395 turns.**
- Battles last only 1–2 turns per day, which is coarse. Night has no special rules. Date-based events cannot be used, so everything is keyed to turn numbers.

**Richmond deadline (user's hard end), any option:**
- At the start, roll a hidden "Lee summons Jackson" date tied to McClellan's Peninsula campaign. Historical anchors: Seven Pines 31 May; Jackson left the Valley via Brown's Gap on 18 June (NPS). Suggested window: about 1–25 June.
- Progressive messages from Richmond (McClellan landing, nearing Richmond, the call itself). The date can shorten if Union forces threaten Richmond more, or extend if Jackson draws off McDowell's corps.
- Victory: after the call, Jackson's army exits east through a pass (Brown's/Rockfish/Swift Run exit hexes) **without having lost the Valley**, i.e. while holding the Valley major VPs (Staunton, Harrisonburg, Winchester…).

How each idea maps to Hex of Steel:

| Idea | Native HoS | Needs Harmony |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day turns, turn limit | Yes (days per turn, turn limit) | – |
| Variable turn length, hour clock | No | Yes (NextTurn hook, MP scaling) |
| Night encampment turn | Partial: a "night" turn by convention, no rules | Rules: MP, visibility 0, combat penalty, detection roll |
| Hidden random deadline | Approximate: an event with a chance % rolled each turn after "not before X" gives a random date | An exact rolled date and adjustable shortening/extension |
| Progressive messages | Yes (message events to a target country) | Messages conditioned on game state |
| Shorten/extend from Valley situation | No | Yes |
| Exit east through passes | No exit mechanic. Approximation: exit-hex VPs or reinforcement tiles plus an event | Yes: count units on exit hexes, remove them, score |
| "Not lost the Valley" | Yes: hold the major VPs | – |
| Union reinforcements through gaps on dates | Yes (spawn events, reinforcement tiles) | Dependence on travel time |

## 7. Minor gaps: Keys', Fisher's, Jarman's
- **Jarman's Gap: KEEP.**
  - Historical role: Virginia historical marker JD-14 (1949, hmdb.org m=16644) says "In June 1862 part of Jackson's army, moving to join Lee at Richmond, used this passage". It lay on the old Three Notch'd Road.
  - Campaign value: a second "leave for Richmond" exit next to Brown's and Rockfish.
  - Confidence: RELIABLE_SECONDARY. The marker is a secondary source; check it against the Official Records.
- **Fisher's (Milam's) Gap: KEEP as a minor route.**
  - Historical role: on 21 April 1862 Hotchkiss scouted "Milham's or Fisher's Gap" on the New Market–Madison Court House road, at Jackson's order, while looking for a concealed flanking road. His sketches 105/106 and Valley map No. 89 label it "Milam's or Fisher's Gap" and "Grave's Gap" (from his journal as quoted at milaminvirginia.com/milams-gap; check the Hotchkiss originals at the Library of Congress).
  - NPS says Jackson's Corps used the Fisher's Gap route in **November 1862**, which is outside our window.
  - Campaign value: a real April 1862 reconnaissance route and plausible alternate crossing between Thornton's and Swift Run.
  - Confidence: PROVISIONAL for 1862 troop use.
- **Keys' Gap: DROP or keep as decorative edge road.**
  - Historical role: my search found no documented role in the March–June 1862 Valley campaign.
  - Campaign value: its sector (Harpers Ferry–Snicker's) is already covered by Harpers Ferry and Snicker's.
  - Verdict: keep the road on the map for geography but no reinforcement exit, unless a source turns up.
  - Confidence: UNKNOWN.
- **Brown's and Rockfish (confirmed by NPS):** "May 3 … Jackson's army departs the Valley via Brown's Gap"; "May 4 … returns … by rail, from Mechum's River Station via Rockfish Gap to Staunton"; 18 June, the march to Richmond via the Brown's Gap Turnpike (nps.gov/articles/000/jackson1862-valley-campaign.htm; nps.gov/articles/shenandoah-civil-war-connection.htm).
