# Historical veterancy and command ratings, Valley Campaign 1862

Historical judgements on a plain 1-5 scale (1 = very poor, 3 = typical for the theatre in 1862, 5 = outstanding). **These are not game stats.** Hex of Steel has no per-unit morale (morale is a global option). Quartermaster maps these ratings to unit experience/rank and commander radius/bonus.

Data: `historical_data/command_ratings_1862.csv`, `historical_data/unit_ratings_1862.csv`; sources SRC050-SRC055 added to `sources_1862.csv`. Rating dates: Kernstown 1862-03-23, McDowell 05-08, Front Royal 05-23, Winchester 05-25, Cross Keys/Port Republic 06-08. A unit is rated only on dates it was in the campaign.

## Method and evidence rules
- OR pages (SRC009 = OR I/12/1, SRC010 = OR I/12/3, SRC050 = OR I/5) were read in the archive.org djvu OCR text and located by running heads. Where an OCR running head was missing, the page range between the neighbouring heads is given.
- I did not view any pages of Cozzens (SRC003), Tanner (SRC051), Robertson (SRC052), Krick (SRC053), the Virginia Regimental Histories (SRC054) or the Conscription Act text (SRC055). They are cited `p. UNVERIFIED`, and a row that rests on them is never `verified`.
- `verified` = the core of the judgement rests on an OR page I viewed. `probable` = an OR page plus a secondary work with an unverified page. `provisional` = thin evidence; the row needs research.
- The validator (`python src/oob.py`, check 16) enforces the 1-5 range, known IDs, the confidence vocabulary, the source-token format and that every `verified` row has a page.

## Balance notes
- **Jackson:** at Kernstown he attacked on a report that Union infantry at Winchester 'did not exceed four regiments' (OR 12/1 p.381). In January his friction with Loring over Romney ended in a resignation letter (OR 5 p.1053). At Port Republic on June 8 his cavalry pickets fled and Carroll's raiders entered the village (OR 12/1 p.713). Ewell fought Cross Keys, and the June 9 attack went in piecemeal until Taylor took the Coaling (pp.713-715). He could not organize Ashby's cavalry (OR 12/3 pp.878-880). Garnett's relief and arrest are cited only from secondary works (pages unverified).
- **Union:** Shields was wounded on Mar 22 (p.338). Kimball then won Kernstown in the field. Banks's retreat saved all but 55 of nearly 500 wagons (p.551), though Winchester's stores were lost. Tyler held the Lewis house-Coaling line against Winder (pp.695-698). Frémont's march was slowed by rain, swollen streams and missing forage, with near-mutiny over privation (pp.5-11). Blenker's division had gone 38 days without tents (pp.5-6). Western Virginia service in 1861 for the Shields, Milroy and Schenck regiments is a secondary claim; regiment-level citations are pending.
- **Confederate April 1862 reorganization:** the Conscription Act of 16 Apr 1862 held 12-month volunteers in service and had them re-elect their company officers (SRC055, page unverified). For the cohesion effect I found only the general complaint in OR 12/3 pp.832-833 (Mar 21 1862) about the 'demoralized and disorganized condition of our Virginia troops' and conflicting reorganization laws. Valley-specific effects are cited from Tanner/Robertson (pages unverified), so the McDowell-date rows are provisional.
- **1st Maryland (CS) mutiny of May 1862:** I found no OR page for it, so it rests on Cozzens/Tanner (pages unverified) and is marked probable. B. T. Johnson's own report (pp.817-818) covers only May 29-June 9.

## Regiments and batteries

216 rows: 95 of 96 regiments and 30 of 34 batteries. Confidence: {'provisional': 46, 'verified': 153, 'probable': 17}.

Method: a regiment starts from its parent brigade's rating on that date (parent taken from `assignments_1862.csv`). It is moved by at most one point only where an OR page I viewed says something about that regiment's conduct. Casualty figures were read from page images of the archive.org scan, because the tables are garbled in the OCR text: Kernstown OR 12/1 p.346 (Union) and p.384 (Confederate); McDowell p.462 (Union) and p.476 (Confederate); Banks's retreat pp.553-554; Ewell's division May 23-25 p.780; Cross Keys pp.664-665 (Union); Port Republic p.690 (Union); Cross Keys and Port Republic pp.717-718 (Confederate). A casualty figure alone does not move a rating; it is recorded in `basis` for Quartermaster. Rows with no regiment-specific evidence say 'inherits ...' and are `provisional`.

Dates rated: Confederate Valley army units on every date they appear in `assignments_1862.csv`. Shields's division only 03-23 and 06-08 (it was east of the Blue Ridge in May). Banks's division 05-25, plus 05-23 for the Front Royal garrison. Frémont's units 05-08 and 06-08.

## Crutchfield start date (note only; commanders_1862.csv and brigade_commands_1862.csv unchanged)
- The repo dates CMD_CRUTCHFIELD as chief of artillery from 1862-03-23. No Kernstown report I read (OR 12/1 pp.379-401) mentions a chief of artillery: Jackson names the batteries by their captains (pp.381-382).
- Jackson's McDowell report (OR 12/1 p.470, located by OCR running head) says 'Colonel Crutchfield, chief of artillery, did not have an opportunity of bringing his command into action on the 8th', so he was in post by 8 May 1862. His own report covers May 23-June 9 (p.724), and Jackson's report praises him (p.707).
- Recommendation: change the start to 'by 1862-05-08 (exact appointment date UNVERIFIED; secondary works put it in April)' and treat 03-23 as unsupported. The Kernstown artillery had no chief-of-artillery commander counter. His command ratings are dated 05-25 and 06-08 only.
